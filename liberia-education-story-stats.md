# The Story of Liberian Education: Key Statistics for CECE's Dream

*Source: Liberia Education Statistics Report 2021-22, Republic of Liberia Ministry of Education (EMIS). Data from the Annual School Census 2021-22.*

---

## The Story Arc

A nation of children. A system in crisis. A generation we can still reach.

---

## Chapter 1: The Setting — A Country Built on Children, Held Back by History

The story starts with who Liberia is. These numbers establish urgency without statistics fatigue.

- **Liberia's population in 2021: 5.18 million.** Over **40% of the population is of school-going age**, and that share is expected to keep growing for most of this century.
- **Liberia is one of the poorest countries in the world** — Human Development Index of 0.48, GDP of $3.5 billion.
- **Three crises in a single generation** wrecked the education system: civil war (1989–2003), Ebola (2014–2016), and COVID-19 (2020+). Schools closed for years at a time. Many never recovered.
- **Less than 30% of Liberians have access to electricity** — and less than 9% in rural areas. Only 22% have internet access.
- During COVID, when education moved online globally, most Liberian children had no way to log on.

> **The takeaway:** This isn't a country that failed its children. It's a country whose children inherited the cost of disasters they didn't cause.

---

## Chapter 2: The Children Who Aren't There

The single most powerful storytelling angle: how few Liberian children are actually in school at the right time.

- **Only 46.3% of primary-school-aged children are enrolled in primary school.** More than half of Liberia's youngest learners are missing from the classroom.
- **Only 20.7% of junior-secondary-aged children are enrolled in junior secondary.** Roughly **4 out of every 5 middle-school-aged children are not in middle school.**
- **Only 17.1% of senior-secondary-aged children are enrolled in senior secondary.** **More than 8 out of 10 high-school-aged kids are out of high school.**
- **Only 8.8% of Liberian children enter Grade 1 at the correct age** (Net Intake Rate). Nine out of ten children either start late, start in the wrong grade, or never start at all.
- **Only ~10% of Liberian children complete primary school** — 10% of boys and 11% of girls (EMIS 2021-22 Annual School Census). The most basic level of education ends for 9 out of 10 children before it's finished.

> **The hook:** "In Liberia, only 1 in 10 children begins first grade on time. Only 2 in 10 teenagers are in middle school. Only 1 in 6 are in high school."

---

## Chapter 3: The Over-Age Crisis — The Children Who Are There, but Years Behind

This is the most striking story in the data: kids ARE in school, just not at the age they're supposed to be.

- **42.5% of preschool / Early Childhood Education students are over-age** — 215,889 children.
- **38.1% of primary school students are over-age** — 218,995 children. That's nearly **4 in 10 primary-school kids** sitting in classrooms with younger children.
- **64.9% of junior secondary students are over-age** — 128,253 students. **Nearly 2 of every 3 middle-school students are too old for their grade.**
- **61.4% of senior secondary students are over-age** — 81,980 students.

> **The visceral image:** A 16-year-old sitting in a 4th-grade class, embarrassed, missing the friendships, milestones, and opportunities her age-mates in other countries take for granted.

---

## Chapter 4: The Quality Crisis — Even When Kids Are in School, the Learning Isn't There

- **Half of all Liberian teachers — 50.1%, or 35,799 teachers — have no teaching certificate at all.** This is illegal under Liberia's own 2011 Education Reform Act, but enforcement has collapsed.
- **In Early Childhood Education (the foundation of literacy), there are 52 students for every qualified teacher.**
- **Classrooms are dangerously overcrowded.** The national average is 25 students per classroom against an international standard of 15–20. In some counties — like Bomi — there are **66 ECE students per classroom.**
- **Senior secondary classrooms have 38 students each on average.**

### Textbooks — or the Lack of Them

- **Junior secondary students share textbooks at devastating ratios:** 18 students per math textbook, 21 per science textbook, 22 per social studies textbook.
- **In senior secondary, 32 students share one math textbook and 29 share one English textbook.**
- In faith-based senior schools, **65 students share a single English textbook.**

> **The line that lands:** "In a Liberian high school, a single math textbook is shared by 32 students. In some schools, by 65."

---

## Chapter 5: Schools Without the Basics

What's missing from a Liberian school is often the things American donors would consider non-negotiable.

- **Only 8% of all schools have piped drinking water.** 53% rely on hand-pumps. Some still drink from creeks.
- **In community schools, only 6% have piped water; 54% rely on hand-pumps; some have no water source at all.**
- Many schools don't have flush toilets — children walk to pit latrines or have no toilets at all.
- **Most schools have no school feeding program**, despite extensive evidence that school meals dramatically increase enrollment, attendance, and completion.
- **109 schools in Liberia can only be reached by canoe.**
- **Only 11% of public schools have a functional School Management Committee.** Oversight, accountability, and parental involvement are largely absent.
- **240 schools (4%) reported cases of school-related gender-based violence** during the census year.

---

## Chapter 6: The Girls and Children Left Furthest Behind

- **In Senior Secondary classrooms, only 3.8% of teachers are women** — down from 9.6% just two years earlier. Girls have almost no female role models in the classroom by the time they reach high school.
- **In Grand Kru and Rivercess counties, there are zero female senior-secondary teachers.**
- **Only 0.2% of all students are recorded as having disabilities** — a number the Ministry of Education itself calls misleadingly low. The report explicitly states: *"nearly all school-aged children with disabilities in Liberia are out of school."*

## Methodology Note

All statistics are drawn from the **Liberia Education Statistics Report 2021-22**, published by the Ministry of Education of the Republic of Liberia in October 2023, prepared by the Education Management Information System (EMIS) Division with support from the World Bank, Global Partnership for Education, UNICEF, and UNIDO. Figures reflect the Annual School Census conducted across all 16 education counties of Liberia.
